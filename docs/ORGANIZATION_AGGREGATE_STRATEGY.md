# Organization Aggregates Strategy

**Status:** Strategy, not started · August 15, 2026

## The ask

Working with Estée Lauder means people in New York offices and people at a plant in Japan. Two
things need to be true:

1. Pull everything relevant from interactions with **anyone related to the company**, across
   sites and org units.
2. Store information about **the company itself** — public investor data, research — that ties
   to no person at all.

## Bottom line

**Do not add a Company primitive.** `Group` already is one, and it is closer to complete than it
looks: `groupType` includes `corporation` and `employer`, `PersonGroup` carries role and start/end
dates, `PlaceGroup` has an `employer_location` relationship type, `GroupGroup` gives hierarchy,
and `Event` already tags to groups. Estée Lauder is a Group; the NY office and the Japan plant are
Places affiliated to it.

Four gaps stand between that and the two things above. Three are small; one is the real problem.

## What production actually looks like

I assumed the problem was dirty `Person.company` strings needing a messy backfill. Measured
against the live database, it is not:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| People | 7,376 |
| …with a `company` value | **4** |
| `PersonGroup` membership edges | **2**, across 1 group |
| Groups | 612 — of which **611 are `groupType: corporation`** |
| `PlaceGroup` edges | 64, **all** `corporate_parent` |

The top "corporations" are Amazon, Uber, Apple, Uber Eats, Target, United Airlines, and none of
them has a single member.

So there is almost nothing to migrate, and one thing to be careful about.

### The real problem: `corporation` already means something else

Those 611 Groups are **merchant brands generated by the spend/places pipeline** — the
`corporate_parent` rollup that lets the Places map collapse individual Starbucks locations under
one brand at low zoom. They are machine-generated, member-less, and working as designed.

`groupType: corporation` therefore already means *"a brand that owns places I spend money at."*
Filing Estée Lauder there drops a curated business relationship into a bucket of 611 merchant
records, and every company view then has to distinguish them by heuristic.

**Use a distinct group type for relationship organizations, and leave the merchant rollup alone.**
`employer` exists in the enum and is entirely unused, so it is available immediately with no
migration — but it is a poor fit for a client or partner. The better move is a small additive
enum value (`client`, or `organization` if you want one bucket for employer/client/partner), and
to have the company-dossier surface filter on that rather than on `corporation`.

Note the consequence: Estée Lauder may legitimately exist **twice** — once as a merchant group if
you spend money there, once as a relationship organization. That is not a bug, but the two should
eventually be linked rather than merged, since they answer different questions.

### `PersonGroup` is unused, not wrong

Two edges in the entire database. There is no existing practice to migrate — this is a greenfield
habit to establish, which is far easier than fixing bad data. `Person.company` (4 rows) can be
folded in and demoted to a display cache in an afternoon.

The design work below still stands. What changes is the effort: **step 1 is nearly free**, and the
risk shifts from data cleanup to namespace discipline.

## Time-scoping is the subtle part

`PersonGroup` has `startDate` and `endDate`, and using them correctly is not optional.

Someone leaves Estée Lauder for L'Oréal in 2024. Their 2022 interactions were Estée Lauder
interactions and must stay in the EL aggregate. Their 2025 interactions must not.

So membership joins are **time-aware**: an interaction counts toward a company when its
`timestamp` falls inside that person's membership window. Join on membership alone and you either
lose history the moment someone moves, or wrongly credit their new employer's conversations to
the old one. Both failures are quiet.

## Where each kind of information lives

Three different shapes of company information, three different homes. Getting this split right is
what keeps it queryable.

| Shape | Example | Home | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Stable identifiers** | ticker, exchange, CIK, fiscal year end | 1:1 profile extension on Group | One value per company, rarely changes |
| **Time series** | market cap, revenue, headcount | `State` on the Group | Already polymorphic and dated |
| **Narrative** | earnings-call takeaways, "the Japan plant is being retooled" | `GroupNote` | Many per company, appendable, human prose |

### Stable identifiers — extend the Group

`SupplierProfile` already does exactly this: a 1:1 typed extension of `Group` carrying
purchasing-specific fields. A `CompanyProfile` in the same shape is the precedent-following move
for ticker, exchange, CIK, IR URL, fiscal year end.

### Time series — `State`, no schema change needed

`State` is polymorphic (`entityType`, `entityId`) with `recordedAt`, and `StateDefinition` is
already scoped by `entityType`, so Group states are typed separately from Person states.
`packages/domain/file-evidence.ts` already promotes States onto Groups, so the path is proven.

Market cap at a date, revenue by quarter, headcount over time all fit with **no migration** — and
they inherit the existing history and provenance behaviour for free.

### Narrative — a `GroupNote`, mirroring `PlaceNote`

`Group.notes` is a single `String`, which cannot hold a running research file. And the existing
`Note.groups` relation is the **reverse of `Group.sourceNoteId`** — provenance recording that a
Group was *derived from* a Note. It is not "notes about this group", and using it that way would
corrupt the provenance meaning.

`PlaceNote` is the proven pattern: dedicated join, body, optional event link, actor, GraphEvent.
`GroupNote` is the same shape. Support table, not a primitive — same status as `PlaceNote`.

## Fourth gap: `Interaction` has no `groupId`

Interactions attach to Person, Event, and Place. So an interaction reaches a company only
*through a person*, which means anything with no human counterparty has nowhere to go:

- an investor-relations email from the company
- sitting in on an earnings call
- submitting an RFP to the company

Add a nullable indexed `groupId`. Small, additive, and it closes the "company itself" half of the
ask on the interaction side.

## Places vs. Groups: don't build two hierarchies

The tempting mistake is making "EL New York" both a Place and a Group. Then they drift.

**Places are locations. Groups are org units.**

- NY office → **Place**, affiliated to the EL Group via `PlaceGroup(employer_location)`
- Japan plant → **Place**, same
- "EL Japan R&D" as a team with its own identity and its own people → **child Group** via
  `GroupGroup`

Create a Group for a site only when a distinct *set of people* has an identity there, not because
a building exists.

## What the aggregate query becomes

"Everything about Estée Lauder" is a union over five paths, each exact rather than string-matched:

1. **Direct** — Interactions with `groupId = EL`
2. **Via people** — Interactions whose `personId` has a `PersonGroup` to EL **whose window
   contains the interaction timestamp**
3. **Via child groups** — recurse `GroupGroup` so EL Japan and EL North America roll up
4. **Via places** — Events and Interactions at Places affiliated to EL through `PlaceGroup`
5. **Company-level** — `State` rows where `entityType='Group'` and `entityId = EL`, plus
   `GroupNote` rows

Spend follows for free: `Interaction.amount` over the same union answers "what have I spent with
Estée Lauder", which is the same shape Places already uses for `stats.totalSpend`.

Path 4 needs a judgement call worth making explicitly: an interaction at the Japan plant with
someone from a *different* company is arguably not an EL interaction. Suggest treating
place-derived matches as a **weaker tier** — included in a "related" view, excluded from
"interactions with EL people" — rather than silently merging the two.

## Phasing

0. **Carve the namespace.** Add a relationship group type (`client`/`organization`) so curated
   companies never mix with the 611 merchant rollups. One additive enum value; everything else
   depends on it.
1. **`PersonGroup` as source of truth.** Only 4 `Person.company` rows to fold in and 2 existing
   edges, so this is establishing a habit rather than a migration. Demote the column to a display
   cache.
2. **Time-aware membership join**, as a single reusable query helper so every surface scopes the
   same way rather than each reinventing it.
3. **`Interaction.groupId`** — nullable, indexed, additive.
4. **`GroupNote`** — mirror `PlaceNote` including its GraphEvent and actor handling.
5. **`CompanyProfile`** — mirror `SupplierProfile` for ticker/exchange/CIK/IR URL.
6. **Company dossier surface** — the union query above, and an assistant tool over it.

Steps 0–3 make the aggregate correct. Steps 4–6 make the company itself a first-class thing you
can read and write.

Because `PersonGroup` is effectively empty, the practical bottleneck is **getting membership
recorded at all**, not schema. Assistant tools (`create_group`, `add_person_to_group`) and
extraction from files — the file-intelligence pipeline already produces `FileEntityMention` rows
carrying employer hints — matter more here than any query work.

## Open questions

1. **What should the relationship group type be called?** `employer` is free today but wrong for a
   client or partner. One broad `organization` value keeps it simple; separate `client`/`employer`
   values make filtering sharper later.
2. **Should the assistant get `create_group` and `add_person_to_group`?** Both are additive and fit
   the Safe tier, and given how empty `PersonGroup` is, the assistant may be the most practical way
   to populate it. Membership edits do change how every future aggregate reads, so this is arguably
   a Confirm-tier decision.
3. **Do you want competitor/customer relationships between companies?** `GroupGroup` currently
   models hierarchy. Non-hierarchical relationships need a relationship type, as `PlaceGroup` has.
4. **What happens when a merchant group and a relationship group are the same company?** Linking
   them would let "what have I spent with Estée Lauder" and "who do I know there" resolve to one
   view; merging them would confuse a machine-generated rollup with a curated record.
